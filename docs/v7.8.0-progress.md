# ManagerStory V7.8.0 — development

Branch `dev/v7.8.0`, started 2026-10-16 from `main` (V7.7.0), after the user's review of V7.7.0 on the iPhone (recording 16 Oct 21:37 and three screenshots). The feedback is split into small packages, each reviewed on the phone:

1. Visible errors (this package): keeper flying in close-ups, keeper standing like a picture, net not reacting, kickoff without a pass, opponents in the box at goal kicks, no attack direction.
2. Team shape: players gathering on the touchline, the pitch looking empty, everyone running somewhere else, players running round the ball on a through pass.
3. Engine decisions: back passes with space ahead, long passes from the forward line back to defence, shooting towards the touchline with an empty goal, unexplained yellow and red cards, offside logic.
4. Movement quality: dribbles, challenges, saves.

## Package 1 — visible errors

- Header jump (the "flying" player in close-ups): the recorded header motion was lifted another 0.4 m, so the boots were ~0.9 m off the grass (measured over a whole match: every header). The extra lift is now 0.12 m (boots ~0.6 m, a strong real jump).
- Keeper set position: the stance was one frozen pose. It is now a 1.4 s cycle — bouncing on the toes, weight shifting from foot to foot, hands up and loose — each keeper at his own phase.
- Goal net: the ball used to fly through the net. The net (2 m deep, back wall 1.6 m, roof sloping from the bar, side panels) now catches the ball: it loses its pace and drops inside the goal. Where it hits, the mesh bulges back and ripples for about a second (stronger for a harder shot); the frame never moves.
- Kickoff: the taker used to start dribbling straight from the centre spot. The 3D view now plays the kickoff as a pass: a short exchange with the nearest team-mate, as the 2D pitch already shows it. (Making the engine itself pass the kickoff to a team-mate was tried; it changed the ball's position at the start of every half and after every goal, which the 2D presentation and its tests are built on, so it was rolled back for now.)
- Goal kicks: opponents leave the penalty area until the kick is taken (engine placement and 3D view).
- Attack direction: a chip at the top of the pitch shows our team's colour and an arrow pointing the way we attack on screen (it turns with the camera and changes at half time); the radar shows the same arrow.
- Engine: an interception of a restart pass put the interceptor exactly on the intended receiver (two players on one spot). He now cuts in beside him.

Two tests made robust to an early injury pause (they assumed a seed reached minute 15–20 without one).

## "Every match tells the same story" — investigation

User: the match feels like it is always played with the same story. Measured over 20 engine matches:

- The match dice were seeded by the fixture number only (`8800 + fixture × 97`), so every new career's first match was the same match: Anadolu Hisarı–Ormanşehir 2-1, goals at 21', 73', 84', all 528 events identical (both of the user's recordings show this match). A fixture replayed from an older save was also a rerun.
  Fixed: a career made in the app carries a salt (`S.matchSalt`, added to existing careers on load) that is drawn anew at every kickoff; the dice of a match in progress are still saved and restored. Without a salt (automated checks) the seed is the fixture's, as before. Three new careers' first matches: 0-0, 3-1, 1-2.
- Results vary (20 matches: 0-0 to 5-0, 12–26 shots), but the flow does not: 65 of 91 minutes start with "X boş alana koşuyor"; the most common minute is off-ball run → pass → control (187 times in 20 matches); 11,445 commentary lines use only 359 different sentences (names masked), and the 7 most common make up over half. Planned with the engine decisions (package 3): more varied minutes (square and back passes for a reason, switches, long balls, keeping the ball) and more commentary sentences.

## Package 2 — team shape

The user: players gather on the touchline and the pitch looks empty (like 6 against 6); everyone runs somewhere else; on a through ball the others run round the ball.

What was measured (whole match, 3D view, fixture 3): each team spread over ~46 m of length and ~48 m of width with ~13 m to the nearest team-mate; the engine's own minute positions are the same (49 × 49 m). On corners the 3D view could show an empty box: the engine arranges the players for a restart within the minute, but the view only knew the positions at the end of the minute.

Changes:
- Restarts: the engine's arrangement for a restart now travels with the `restartPlayers` event (positions only; no new random draws, results unchanged). As soon as the ball is out, the 3D players head for it (jogging when far), and the restart is taken once they are nearly in place (up to 4.5 s for a corner, 1.2 s for a throw-in). Corners now show 8–9 players in the box; at goal kicks no opponent is in the box.
- Within a minute the 3D players move from the last engine keyframe towards the next one as the minute's events are played, instead of all heading at once for where they will be a minute later.
- Around the ball (3D view): the team without the ball squeezes towards it (mostly across, a little along the pitch), the team with it offers shorter options. Team width 47.5 → 40.5 m, nearest team-mate 12.7 → 11.3 m.
- A ball played into space is contested: the nearest opponent sets off after it as well (he arrives late, the engine has decided who wins it), so nobody jogs past a ball in play.
- Tried and rolled back: making the engine's block narrower and shifting it to the ball side. It made the shape only slightly tighter (49 → 45 m) but cut scoring from 2.5 to 1.85 goals a match; the squeeze is done in the 3D view instead (2D and results unchanged).
