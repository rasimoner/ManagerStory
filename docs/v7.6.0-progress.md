# ManagerStory V7.6.0 — development

Branch `dev/v7.6.0`, started 2026-10-15 from `main` (V7.5.0).

## Career: decision tray

The day's decision (title, text, choices, "Günü Tamamla") is a fixed tray above the bottom navigation instead of a card far down the page; the page scrolls behind it and is padded so nothing hides under it. The header collapses it to one line (saved in `S.ui.trayCollapsed`).

## Engine: shot/pass decisions, through balls, real offside

Engine decision change (results of old seeds differ; identical inputs stay deterministic).

- `shotValueAt` (pitch-v73.js): what a shot is worth from a point, on a realistic distance/angle curve (≈ .33 at 6 m, .17 at 11 m, .07 at 18 m, .03 at 25 m), scaled by finishing and pressure. Used to decide only; the shot outcome model is unchanged.
- Action loop: shooting weight follows that value; in a good position the carrier's pass weight drops and backward options are discounted.
- Chance creation (`chanceV73`): the carrier shoots only if no team-mate is clearly better placed. A forward waiting on the offside line is played into his run behind the defence (pass into the running path; he arrives where the ball is played). Too far and nobody better: the carrier drives forward (real dribble, can be lost) up to twice. From beyond ~30 m a shot is a rare gamble (more for good finishers or a "shoot" order). The final ball can be cut out. Cautious mentality discounts risky final balls more than bold.
- Offside: the random 6.5% call is gone. Attackers wait on the line (level with the second-last defender or the ball); poor movers drift. A pass is flagged only if the receiver is beyond the second-last defender at the pass, in the opponent half and ahead of the ball; because players move a minute at a time, the flag probability grows with how far beyond he is and falls with his off-ball skill. Passers mostly avoid a clearly offside team-mate (vision).
- Minute-level chance frequency raised (.16 → .34 × tactical frequency) to keep shot volume now that long-range shots are rare.

## New 3D view: recorded motions (sliding tackle, header, celebration)

Three full-body motions recorded by Quaternius (CC0, see `docs/open-assets.md`) are retargeted onto our skeleton by `tools/build-rig.mjs`, which now reads GLB as well as glTF and joins clip segments with a short crossfade:

- `tackle` — Slide_Start + Slide_Loop + Slide_Exit: drop, slide on the hip, get up into a run.
- `header` — Jump_Start + Jump_Land; the flight itself (about 0.4 m) is lifted by the runtime, with a nod through the ball at contact.
- `cheer` — Celebration (both arms up) for the goal scorer.

The library's "Kick" is a martial-arts kick, so passes and shots stay on our own keyframes.

Runtime (`newview/runtime.js`): `startMotion` plays a recorded motion over the locomotion blend (fade in/out); `slideAt` steers a slide so the leading boot reaches where the ball will be on the contact frame; `headerAt` times a jump so the forehead meets the ball at a given point. Lofted balls now allow for air drag, so a cross really arrives where and when it was aimed.

Director (`newview/director.js`):
- About two in five challenges go to ground (most of those that end in a foul). A won slide pokes the ball away and the defender collects it when he is back up; a lost one is knocked past the sliding leg. A defender who never gets close stays on his feet.
- A corner that the engine turns into a header shot: the target times his jump onto the cross and nods it at the engine's aim; the nearest marker goes up a touch late. A corner the engine clears or turns into a scramble: both go up, the winner heads it to where the engine says the ball went.
- The goal scorer celebrates with the recorded motion.
- After the final whistle the view still plays the last few actions (it used to freeze on them when it was behind the clock).

Measured in headless Chromium (full matches, fixtures 3 and 7, 0 page errors, every engine event played, 0 stalls, scores equal to the engine): the forehead meets the ball within 0.05–0.13 m; the sliding boot within 0.03–0.08 m on most slides.

## Engine: corners

Most real corners come from defenders. New corner sources (pitch-v73.js), each a real restart through the existing corner sequence:

- a cross cut out in the final 30% (60%) or another ball into the box cut out (35%) goes behind off the defender;
- a lost aerial contest in the box is headed behind (30%);
- a challenge won on the byline (final 20%) goes off the defender (40% when the dribbler is stopped, 35% when pressed);
- a missed shot through a crowded area (nearest marker within 7) can take a deflection behind (up to 40%).

Measured over the same 68 engine matches (V7.6.0 dev before → after):

Real top-flight football has about 10 corners a match; the rest of the gap is crossing volume (about 10 crosses a match here against 20+), left for a later step.

## Full time

- Engine (`playLastAttack`, pitch-v73.js, called from the 90th-minute tick): the referee does not blow in the middle of a promising attack. If an attacker has the ball in the final third and no shot came that minute, the move is played out first (up to five chance steps; it ends at a shot, a turnover or a player shielding it by the corner flag). Over 68 matches: matches ending with an attacker on the ball in the final third 29% → 15% (the rest end after a rebound, a long goal kick or shielding at the flag); shots in the 90th minute 0.35 → 0.50 a match; goals unchanged (2.56).
- New 3D view: at the final whistle play stops where it is and everyone eases to a stop instead of freezing mid-stride.

## Leg opening at jogging pace

The step rate stays as it is (compared +4/+8/+12%; the `cadence` option remains, default 1).

The recorded jog clip, used for every running speed below a sprint, opens the legs wider than the sprint clip itself: feet up to 1.24 m apart and the thigh swinging 77° (sprint: 1.13 m, 71°). `createRuntime({ stride })` reins in the jog clip's share of the pose: hips, knees and arms swing less far either side of the jog cycle's average pose, and a leg that would dig into the grass lifts the body by that much. The sprint keeps its full opening. Default 0.75 (feet about 1.02 m apart, thigh about 63°); `?bacak=1` shows the recorded jog.

## Walk, jog, run, sprint

The engine places players once a minute; it never chose a gait. The view used to pick a speed from distance alone, so players stood at their spot and then all jogged to the next one. Measured over a full browser match (outfield players, sampled every 0.2 s): standing 67%, walking 5%, jogging 17%, running 11%, sprinting 0%.

Now (director):
- Between engine minutes the block slides with the ball as it actually moves (each player's minute position is offset by part of the ball's movement since then), so players keep drifting at a walk.
- Speed is chosen from the distance to go with hysteresis: stand, walk 1.5, jog 3.3, run 5.2, sprint 7.4 m/s; dead ball caps at a jog, keepers at a run.
- Sprints where football has them: a run in behind, closing down from distance, chasing a long ball, the scorer's celebration run.
- With the ball: driving into open grass (nobody within 12 m) at up to 6.4 m/s, slowing to 3.8 m/s with a man close.

After (with the engine changes below, same fixture): standing 34%, walking 44%, jogging 18%, running 3%, sprinting 2%. Standing includes dead-ball stoppages.

## Engine: shorter passes, a more compact team

The ball was played long: mean pass 31 m, half of all passes over 30 m (real football: about 17–20 m, 10–12% long). Two causes: the team block spanned goal line to goal line whatever the ball's position, and the passer weighed every team-mate about equally however far.

- The formation block now moves up and down the pitch with the ball (45% of its movement) and is 27% shorter; tactical and individual offsets are added on top unchanged.
- The passer prefers nearer options (weight falls with distance; better vision and a direct style look further, a short style less far).
- Set-piece recovery runs are capped by pace in any direction (a diagonal used to be up to 41% faster).

Same 34 engine matches, before → after: mean pass 31.4 → 24.9 m; passes over 30 m 51% → 31%; under 15 m 13% → 24%; completion 71% → 75%; team depth 56 → 51 m; goals 2.56 → 2.53; shots 20.2 → 17.1; xG 2.64 → 2.16; corners 5.4 → 3.9; offside 4.2 → 3.4.

The rest of the gap is structural: the engine plays about one or two actions a minute (real football: about ten passes), so one engine pass often stands for a short sequence. Closing it means more, shorter actions per minute, which also makes a match longer to watch; left as a separate decision.

Tests: the set-piece recovery test now also treats a new corner's placement like a kickoff (by the laws, not a run) and skips a sample a second corner interrupts. Engine/core 123/123, release gate 62/62.
